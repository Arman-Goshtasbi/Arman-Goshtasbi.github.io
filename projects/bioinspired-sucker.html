Published Research — M.S., University of Twente

Bioinspired stiffness-tunable sucker

A vacuum suction cup that adapts to steep angular surfaces while soft, then stiffens after attachment using granular jamming — no extra actuator or sensor required.

← All projects

Overview

Inspired by the octopus's extrinsic sucker muscles, this design pairs a conventional active suction pad with a soft granular-jammed stalk. Before attachment, the stalk is compliant and bends easily, letting the suction pad conform to steep angled surfaces. Once the pad seals against the surface, the same vacuum source jams the granular particles inside the stalk, sharply increasing its bending stiffness — so the same low-pressure source both attaches the gripper and stiffens the joint, with no separate actuator or sensor needed. The sucker adapted to angles up to 85° with under 0.5 N of force, and in a real-world demo held a water-filled glass carafe on an angled surface, adding 1,028 g of lateral load without slipping.

A. Goshtasbi, A. Sadeghi, "A Bioinspired Stiffness Tunable Sucker for Passive Adaptation and Firm Attachment to Angular Substrates," Frontiers in Robotics and AI, vol. 10, 2023. doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2023.1080015 →
