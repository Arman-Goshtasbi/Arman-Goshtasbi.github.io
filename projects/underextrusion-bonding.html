Published Research — M.S., University of Twente

Underextrusion bonding for soft-rigid hybrids

An FDM 3D printing technique that turns a common printing defect — underextrusion — into a strong, repeatable bond between rigid plastic and soft silicone.

← All projects

Overview

Bonding soft silicone to rigid plastic is a common bottleneck in hybrid soft robots — adhesives are failure-prone and design-limiting. This work turns underextrusion, normally considered a 3D printing defect, into a bonding mechanism: printing a section of a part at a reduced flow rate creates a porous, fibrous structure similar to connective tissue, which silicone can penetrate and interlock with during casting. Across lap shear, peel, and pressure tests, the technique outperformed commercial silicone adhesive by roughly 70–280%, depending on the test and material. We demonstrated the approach with two hybrid grippers: one with a rigid "nail" bonded to a soft finger for picking up small objects like LEDs, and a hexagonal inflatable gripper with rigid panels connected by soft ballooning joints.

A. Goshtasbi, L. Grignaffini, A. Sadeghi, "Bio-inspired 3D Printing Approach for Bonding Soft and Rigid Materials Through Underextrusion," Scientific Reports, vol. 15, p. 4429, 2025. doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-84525-7 →
